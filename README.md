The Eave reimagines architecture through the lens of pandemic crisis, responding to the collapse of New York’s horizontal urban systems—overcrowded hospitals, fractured supply chains, and social isolation that triggered large-scale migration. Conceived as an urban prototype, the tower shifts between normal and emergency states, integrating healthcare, housing, and logistics into a single, continuous infrastructure. It asks how architecture might absorb crisis as part of everyday operation, expanding into the horizontal airspace above the city to reprogram and sustain life when surrounding systems fail. This logic extends into the project’s digital presentation: a website that mirrors the building’s dual states. Vertical scrolling traces the tower’s section and individual movement through stacked programs, while horizontal sliders unfold the expandable emergency eaves—revealing data and narratives that extend outward from the core to accommodate new people, spaces, and modes of living.
